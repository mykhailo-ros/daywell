Five directions

Each direction leans a different way: product, calm, letter, routine, type. All are drawn on a 48-unit grid in black first, with the sage accent as an optional second tone. They're shown here at the sizes they'll really be used.

01

Kept dot

Leans: Product-native, systematic

The streak legend, reduced to two marks: a filled dot for Done and a ring for a kept day. It says “a skip doesn't break it” without words.

Daywell

Works because

  • Built straight from the product's own visual language
  • Reads instantly at 20px
  • Easy to animate: the ring fills in on check-in

Watch out

  • Abstract: needs the name beside it at first
  • Two dots is a familiar shape in tech logos
02

Horizon

Leans: Calm, wellbeing, warm

A sun resting on a line. One day, steady and unhurried. The line under it suggests a routine you stand on.

Daywell

Works because

  • Warmest and most “wellbeing” of the five
  • Simple silhouette that holds up at any size
  • Links to the name: Day

Watch out

  • Sunrise marks are common in wellness
  • Less tied to tracking and streaks
03

Day arc D

Leans: Monogram, confident, letter-led

A rounded D with a sun inside it: the day as a container for one small thing. It's a letter first and a symbol second.

Daywell

Works because

  • Owns the initial, which works well for an app icon
  • Most distinctive outline of the five
  • The dot carries the accent nicely

Watch out

  • The inner dot gets small below 20px
  • More “brand” than “product” in feel
04

Daily loop

Leans: Routine, habit, continuity

An almost-closed ring with today as the dot in the opening. Each check-in closes the loop, and the ring is the routine that comes back every day.

Daywell

Works because

  • Shows the habit loop: repeat, return
  • Very calm, nothing sharp
  • Matches the round check-in control in the app

Watch out

  • Close to “power” and “refresh” icons
  • The meaning needs a moment to land
05

Wordmark

Leans: Typographic, friendly, quiet

No separate symbol. “daywell” in lowercase Figtree, with the double l drawn as two pills, one filled and one outlined, so the streak idea hides inside the name. The app icon uses the lowercase d.

Works because

  • Puts the name front and centre while it's new
  • Matches the UI typeface exactly
  • The pill detail rewards a second look

Watch out

  • The app icon is just a letter, so it's weaker on a busy home screen
  • The pill detail disappears at small sizes

To choose a direction

  1. Pick the one or two that feel most like Daywell. It's fine to like a direction and dislike its drawing.
  2. Say what to keep from the others, for example the warmth of Horizon with the system of Kept dot.
  3. Next round: three refinements of the chosen direction, the wordmark tuned to match, and a trademark search on the shortlist.
